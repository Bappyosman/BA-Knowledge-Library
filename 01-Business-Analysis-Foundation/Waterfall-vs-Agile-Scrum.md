# 🌊 Waterfall vs. Agile/Scrum

Imagine you want to build an **online shopping website**. 🛒💻

There are different ways to manage the software development process.

Two popular approaches are:

🔵 **Waterfall**
🟢 **Agile/Scrum**

---

# 🔵 What Is the Waterfall Model?

The **Waterfall Model** follows a step-by-step process.

The team completes one phase before moving to the next phase.

### Typical Flow:

**Planning → Requirements → Design → Development → Testing → Deployment → Maintenance**

It moves forward like water flowing down a waterfall. 🌊

---

## 🏠 Simple Example: Building a House

Imagine building a house.

### 1️⃣ Planning

Decide what type of house to build.

### 2️⃣ Requirements

Decide:

🏠 Number of rooms
🚪 Number of doors
🪟 Number of windows

### 3️⃣ Design

Create the building plan.

### 4️⃣ Construction

Build the house.

### 5️⃣ Testing

Check whether everything is correct.

### 6️⃣ Delivery

Give the house to the owner.

You generally do not change the entire house design after construction has started.

---

# 💻 Waterfall Example: Software Project

Imagine building an **online banking application**.

### Phase 1: Requirements

The team collects all requirements:

✅ Login
✅ Balance checking
✅ Money transfer
✅ Bill payment

### Phase 2: Design

The complete system is designed.

### Phase 3: Development

Developers build the system.

### Phase 4: Testing

Testers test the complete application.

### Phase 5: Deployment

The application is released.

The project follows a planned sequence.

---

# 🟢 What Is Agile?

**Agile** is a flexible way of developing software.

Instead of planning everything in detail and waiting until the end, the team:

> **Builds a small part, tests it, gets feedback, and improves it.**

### Simple Flow:

**Plan → Build → Test → Get Feedback → Improve → Repeat**

🔁 🔁 🔁

---

# 🍕 Simple Example: Ordering Pizza

Imagine you are making a pizza. 🍕

### Waterfall:

You decide everything first:

🍕 Size
🧀 Cheese
🥩 Toppings
🌶️ Sauce

Then you make the entire pizza.

You taste it at the end.

---

### Agile:

You make a small version first.

Then you ask:

> "Do you like it?"

The customer gives feedback:

> "Add more cheese."

Then you improve the next version.

---

# 🟢 What Is Scrum?

**Scrum** is one of the most popular frameworks used to apply Agile principles.

Scrum divides work into short periods called:

> **Sprints**

A Sprint usually lasts between **1 and 4 weeks**.

During each Sprint, the team:

1️⃣ Plans the work
2️⃣ Develops features
3️⃣ Tests the features
4️⃣ Reviews the work
5️⃣ Improves the process

Then the next Sprint begins.

---

✅ Features are tested

# 👥 Scrum Team

A Scrum Team usually includes:

## 👤 Product Owner

Decides:

🎯 What should be built
📋 Which feature is most important
📊 What provides the most business value

---

## 🧑‍💼 Scrum Master

Helps the team:

🤝 Follow Scrum practices
🚧 Remove obstacles
🔄 Improve the process

---

## 👨‍💻 Developers

Build and test the product.

This can include:

👨‍💻 Developers
🧪 Testers
🎨 Designers

---

# 📅 Scrum Events

## 📝 Sprint Planning

The team decides:

> "What will we work on during this Sprint?"

---

## ☀️ Daily Scrum

A short daily meeting where the team discusses progress.

Common questions:

✅ What did I do yesterday?
🎯 What will I do today?
🚧 Is anything blocking me?

---

## 🎬 Sprint Review

The team shows the completed work to stakeholders.

They receive feedback.

---

## 🔍 Sprint Retrospective

The team discusses:

👍 What went well?
⚠️ What did not go well?
💡 How can we improve?

---

# 📊 Waterfall vs. Agile/Scrum

| Waterfall                               | Agile/Scrum                    |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Step-by-step                            | Iterative                      |
| Requirements usually fixed early        | Requirements can change        |
| Testing often happens after development | Testing happens continuously   |
| Customer feedback may come later        | Regular feedback               |
| Product delivered near the end          | Features delivered gradually   |
| Changes can be difficult                | Changes are expected           |
| Best for stable requirements            | Best for changing requirements |

---

# 🏗️ When Is Waterfall Useful?

Waterfall can be useful when:

✅ Requirements are clear and stable
✅ The project has strict regulations
✅ The project requires detailed documentation
✅ Changes are unlikely
✅ The project has a fixed scope

### Example:

🏗️ Construction projects
🏛️ Government projects
✈️ Some safety-critical systems

---

# 🔄 When Is Agile/Scrum Useful?

Agile/Scrum can be useful when:

✅ Requirements may change
✅ Customer feedback is important
✅ The product needs frequent improvements
✅ The market changes quickly
✅ The team wants to deliver features gradually

### Example:

📱 Mobile applications
🛒 E-commerce platforms
💻 Web applications
🚀 Startups

---

# 👨‍💼 Role of a Business Analyst

### In Waterfall:

A Business Analyst may:

📝 Collect detailed requirements
📄 Prepare BRD/SRS documents
🤝 Get stakeholder approval
📋 Support design and development
🧪 Validate the final product

---

### In Agile/Scrum:

A Business Analyst may:

🔍 Understand business needs
📝 Write User Stories
🎯 Define Acceptance Criteria
📊 Prioritize requirements
🤝 Communicate with stakeholders
💬 Clarify requirements for developers
🧪 Support testing and feedback

---

# 🎯 The Main Difference

### 🔵 Waterfall:

> **Plan everything → Build everything → Test everything → Deliver**

### 🟢 Agile/Scrum:

> **Plan a small part → Build → Test → Get feedback → Improve**

---

## 🧠 Easy Way to Remember

### Waterfall 🌊

Like building a house:

> **Plan first, build later.**

### Agile/Scrum 🔁

Like improving a product step by step:

> **Build a small part, learn from feedback, and improve.**

Neither approach is always the best.

The right choice depends on:

🎯 Project requirements
💰 Budget
⏳ Timeline
🔄 Expected changes
👥 Customer needs

> **Waterfall focuses on following a planned sequence. Agile/Scrum focuses on delivering value in small steps and continuously learning from feedback.** 🚀
