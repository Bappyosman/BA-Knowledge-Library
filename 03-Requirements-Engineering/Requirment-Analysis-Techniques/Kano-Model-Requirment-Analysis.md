📌 **Kano Model: Understanding What Customers Really Want**

When we build a product, we may have many ideas for new features.

But an important question is:

👉 **Which features will actually make customers satisfied?**

Not all features have the same impact on customer satisfaction.

Some features are expected.
Some make customers more satisfied when they perform better.
Some can pleasantly surprise customers.

The **Kano Model** helps us understand how different product features affect customer satisfaction.

It is useful for Business Analysts, Product Managers, and product teams when deciding which features to prioritize.

### 🔴 1. Must-Be Features

These are the basic features customers expect.

Customers may not praise you when these features work properly because they consider them normal.

However, if these features are missing or do not work correctly, customers become very unhappy.

**Example: E-commerce website**

✅ Secure payment
✅ Product delivery
✅ Correct order processing

Customers may not say, "Amazing! The website delivered my order."

They simply expect it.

But if they pay and do not receive their order, they will be very dissatisfied.

👉 **Basic expectation = Must-Be Feature**

### 🟠 2. Performance Features

The better these features perform, the more satisfied customers become.

There is a direct relationship between performance and satisfaction.

**Example: Delivery time**

🚚 Delivery in 7 days = Normal satisfaction
🚚 Delivery in 3 days = More satisfaction
🚚 Delivery in 1 day = Even more satisfaction

👉 **Better performance = Higher satisfaction**

### 🟡 3. Attractive Features

These are features customers do not necessarily expect.

But when they receive them, they become pleasantly surprised and more satisfied.

**Example:**

A customer orders food from a delivery app.

The customer receives:

🍔 The ordered food
🎁 A free dessert

The customer did not expect the free dessert.

That unexpected benefit creates a positive experience.

👉 **Unexpected benefit = Higher satisfaction**

### ⚪ 4. Indifferent Features

These are features that customers do not really care about.

Whether the feature exists or not, it does not make much difference to their satisfaction.

For example, a product may have a small feature that customers rarely use.

The company may spend time and money developing it, but customers may not see any real value.

👉 **No significant impact = Indifferent Feature**

### ❌ 5. Reverse Features

Sometimes, adding a feature can actually make some customers less satisfied.

For example, some users may prefer a simple application.

If we add too many complex features, pop-ups, or complicated steps, the product may become difficult to use.

👉 **More features do not always mean more satisfaction.**

### 📌 A Simple Example: Food Delivery App

🔴 **Must-Be:** The app should deliver the ordered food.

🟠 **Performance:** Faster delivery can increase satisfaction.

🟡 **Attractive:** A surprise free dessert can delight customers.

⚪ **Indifferent:** A feature that customers rarely use.

❌ **Reverse:** Too many unnecessary steps that make ordering difficult.

### 💡 Why is the Kano Model Important?

The Kano Model helps teams understand:

✅ What customers expect
✅ What makes customers more satisfied
✅ What can pleasantly surprise customers
✅ Which features have little value
✅ Which features may create a negative experience

The most important lesson is:

👉 **Customers do not value every feature equally.**

A feature that seems important to the development team may not be important to the customer.

So, before building a feature, we should ask:

**"How will this feature affect customer satisfaction?"**

As a Business Analyst, understanding customer needs is important because the goal is not simply to build more features.

The goal is to build the **right features that create real value for customers.**
