
These questions are a very good starting point for analyzing an **existing retail business using sales data**. I would organize them by **business analysis objective**, not by the order in which they were written.

A good analysis flow is:

**Business Overview → Sales Performance → Product Analysis → Branch Analysis → Customer Analysis → Salesperson Analysis → Inventory Analysis → Time/Seasonality Analysis → Customer Feedback → Delivery Performance → Recommendations**

# 6. Customer Segmentation

You can divide customers into different groups.

### Example

```text
High-Value Customers
        ↓
Regular Customers
        ↓
Occasional Customers
        ↓
One-Time Customers
```

You can use:

### RFM Analysis

RFM means:

* **Recency**: How recently did the customer purchase?
* **Frequency**: How often does the customer purchase?
* **Monetary Value**: How much money does the customer spend?

Example:

| Customer   | Recency | Frequency | Monetary Value |
| ---------- | ------: | --------: | -------------: |
| Customer A |    High |      High |           High |
| Customer B |     Low |    Medium |           High |
| Customer C |    High |       Low |            Low |

This helps identify the most valuable customers.

### Key Metrics

#### Stock Turnover

```text
Stock Turnover =
Cost of Goods Sold / Average Inventory
```

A high turnover usually means the product sells quickly.

A low turnover may indicate:

* Low demand
* Over-purchasing
* Poor pricing
* Poor marketing

## My Recommendation

Your current questions are a good **starting point**, but I would not treat them as the final analysis.

The biggest improvement is to move from simply asking:

> **"What happened?"**

to asking:

> **"Why did it happen?"**

and then:

> **"What should the business do about it?"**

For example:

```text
Data:
Branch A has the highest sales
        ↓
Analysis:
Why does Branch A sell more?
        ↓
Root Cause:
Better location + higher customer traffic
        ↓
Business Recommendation:
Use the same successful strategy in lower-performing branches
```

That is the difference between being only a **Data Analyst** and thinking like a **Business Analyst**.
