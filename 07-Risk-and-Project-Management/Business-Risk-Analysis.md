# 📌 Business Risk Analysis: What Could Go Wrong in a Business?

Imagine you want to open a small lemonade shop. 🍋🥤

You have a good idea, but before starting, you should ask:

🤔 What if people do not buy the lemonade?
🤔 What if the price of lemons increases?
🤔 What if another shop opens nearby?
🤔 What if the machine breaks?
🤔 What if we run out of money?

These are **business risks**.

A **Business Risk Analysis** helps us identify these possible problems before they seriously affect the business.

---

## 🔹 What Is Business Risk Analysis?

In simple words:

> **Business Risk Analysis means finding out what could harm a business, understanding how serious it could be, and planning what to do about it.**

The basic process is:

🔍 **Find the Risk**
⬇️
📊 **Analyze the Risk**
⬇️
⚖️ **Measure Its Impact**
⬇️
🛡️ **Plan a Response**
⬇️
👀 **Monitor the Risk**

---

# 💡 A Simple Business Example

Imagine we want to start an **online marketplace**.

The business could face many risks:

💰 Not enough revenue
👥 Not enough customers
🏪 Sellers may leave the platform
💻 The website may stop working
🔐 Customer data may be stolen
🏢 A competitor may offer a better service
📜 Government rules may change

Business Risk Analysis helps us understand these risks before they become serious problems.

---

# 📌 Step 1: Identify Business Risks

First, we ask:

> **“What could go wrong with this business?”**

There are many types of business risks.

---

## 💰 1. Financial Risk

This happens when a business may lose money or not earn enough money.

### Example:

Imagine you open a restaurant.

You spend:

💵 100,000 BDT on rent
💵 50,000 BDT on equipment
💵 30,000 BDT on staff

But very few customers come.

The business may lose money.

👉 This is a **Financial Risk**.

### Other examples:

* Costs become higher
* Revenue becomes lower
* Customers do not pay
* The business runs out of cash

---

## 👥 2. Market Risk

This happens when customer demand changes.

Imagine you sell winter jackets. 🧥

But this year:

🌡️ The weather is warmer
👥 People do not buy many jackets

Your sales may decrease.

👉 This is **Market Risk**.

### Other examples:

* Customer preferences change
* Demand decreases
* A new trend appears
* The target market is too small

---

## 🏢 3. Competitive Risk

A competitor may offer something better.

Imagine you own a food delivery business.

Then another company offers:

🚚 Faster delivery
💰 Lower prices
🎁 More discounts

Some customers may move to the competitor.

👉 This is **Competitive Risk**.

---

## 💻 4. Technology Risk

Technology may fail or become outdated.

For example:

🖥️ Website stops working
📱 Mobile app crashes
💾 Data is lost
🔗 API stops working
🔐 System has a security problem

For an online business, technical problems can directly affect sales.

---

## 👥 5. People Risk

A business depends on people.

For example:

👨‍💻 An important developer leaves
👩‍💼 A skilled manager resigns
👥 Employees do not have the necessary skills

If one important person leaves, the business may face problems.

---

## 🚚 6. Operational Risk

This happens when the daily business process does not work properly.

Imagine an online store.

A customer:

🛒 Places an order
⬇️
💳 Makes payment
⬇️
📦 Waits for delivery

But the warehouse sends the wrong product.

This is an operational problem.

### Other examples:

* Wrong product delivered
* Inventory information is incorrect
* Supplier fails to deliver
* Customer support is poor

---

## 📜 7. Legal and Regulatory Risk

Businesses must follow laws and regulations.

For example:

📜 Data protection rules
💳 Payment regulations
🧾 Tax requirements
📋 Business licenses

If a business does not follow the law, it may face:

⚠️ Fines
⚠️ Legal problems
⚠️ Business restrictions

---

## 🔐 8. Cybersecurity Risk

Online businesses store important information.

For example:

🔑 Passwords
💳 Payment information
📧 Email addresses
📦 Order details

If hackers access this information, the business and customers can be seriously affected.

👉 This is a **Cybersecurity Risk**.

---

# 📌 Step 2: Understand the Probability

Now we ask:

> **“How likely is this risk to happen?”**

For example:

### Risk: Website Failure

🟢 Low Probability: Unlikely to happen
🟡 Medium Probability: Could happen
🔴 High Probability: Very likely to happen

If the website frequently crashes, the probability is high.

---

# 📌 Step 3: Understand the Impact

Now we ask:

> **“If this happens, how much damage could it cause?”**

### 🟢 Low Impact

Small problem.

Example:

A minor design issue.

### 🟡 Medium Impact

The business loses some time or money.

Example:

A supplier is late for a few days.

### 🔴 High Impact

The business faces serious damage.

Example:

Customer payment data is stolen.

---

# 📊 Risk Score

A simple formula can help us prioritize risks:

> **Risk Score = Probability × Impact**

For example:

### Risk: Customer Data Breach

Probability: **4/5**
Impact: **5/5**

**Risk Score = 4 × 5 = 20**

This is a high-priority risk.

The business should take action quickly.

---

# 📌 Step 4: Decide What to Do About the Risk

After finding a risk, the business must decide how to respond.

## 🛑 1. Avoid the Risk

Change the plan to avoid the risk.

### Example:

A business wants to sell a product in a market with very low demand.

After research, it chooses a different product.

👉 The risk is avoided.

---

## 🛡️ 2. Reduce the Risk

Take action to make the risk less likely or less harmful.

### Example:

Risk:

Only one supplier provides important products.

Solution:

Find two additional suppliers.

Now, if one supplier fails, the business can continue.

👉 The risk is reduced.

---

## 🤝 3. Transfer the Risk

Give responsibility to another organization.

### Example:

A business uses a professional payment provider instead of managing the entire payment system itself.

Some responsibility is transferred to the service provider.

---

## 👀 4. Accept the Risk

Sometimes a risk is small.

The business may decide:

> “This risk is not serious enough to spend a lot of money fixing.”

So the business accepts it and monitors it.

# 📌 Business Risk vs. Project Risk

These two are related, but they are not the same.

### 🏗️ Project Risk

Affects a specific project.

Example:

> “The software project may be delayed.”

### 🏢 Business Risk

Affects the business itself.

Example:

> “The business may lose customers and revenue.”

👉 **Project Risk = Risk to a project**
👉 **Business Risk = Risk to the business**

---

A Business Analyst may help identify:

📉 Business problems
👥 Customer risks
💰 Financial risks
🏢 Competitor risks
💻 Technology risks
📜 Legal risks
🔄 Operational risks

The Business Analyst helps answer:

👉 What could go wrong?
👉 How serious could it be?
👉 How likely is it?
👉 What can the business do about it?

---

## 🎯 The Main Idea

Business Risk Analysis is like checking the weather before going outside. ☂️

You do not know exactly what will happen.

But by looking at possible risks, you can prepare yourself.

A business should ask:

> **“What could harm our business, and what can we do before it happens?”**

That is the basic idea of **Business Risk Analysis**.

🚀 **Good businesses do not wait for every problem to happen. They prepare for possible problems before they become serious.**
